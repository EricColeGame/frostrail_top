## Why Frostrail Crafting Decides Whether Your Run Survives

Frostrail doesn't reward the player who swings hardest — it rewards the one who plans the workbench before the train leaves the station. Frostrail crafting is the spine of every run: it converts scavenged scrap, logs, and cloth into the tools, ammunition, and warm clothing that keep a Penitent Gardener alive between stops. Get the loop wrong and you burn fuel you can't replace; get it right and your armored train becomes a rolling factory that never has to gamble on a loot drop.

It helps to think of Frostrail crafting less as a recipe list and more as a supply chain. Developer material describes benches and refineries that can be bolted onto the train itself, which means your production capacity travels with you. That single design choice changes how you should approach every expedition: you are not just looking for gear, you are looking for inputs.

This guide pulls together what is publicly documented about the system as of the October 2026 pre-release entries on the [Frostrail wiki's weapons, items, and crafting pages](https://frostrail.org/wiki). Where numbers don't exist yet, this article says so instead of guessing — an important habit in a game that is still actively changing.

## The Crafting Stations You Can Bolt to the Train

The clearest confirmed detail about Frostrail crafting is structural: facilities are added to the train as it expands, and they convert recovered resources into survival capacity. Official station names, costs, footprint sizes, and upgrade trees have not been published, so treat any specific blueprint you see online with suspicion.

| Facility | Known or described function | Publication status |
|---|---|---|
| Crafting bench | General equipment and gear production | Confirmed as train-addable; no recipe list |
| Refinery | Processes raw materials into refined goods | Described in developer interviews |
| Assembly Station | Modular firearm assembly and part swapping | Shown in official screenshots |
| Cooking station | Produces food that improves health and other stats | Known to exist |
| Onboard work areas | Implied by workbench and machinery imagery in carriages | Not detailed |

Two practical takeaways follow from that table. First, the Assembly Station is the only station we have actually *seen* in a working state, which makes it the safest reference point for how modular Frostrail crafting will feel. Second, because benches and refineries are train modules rather than fixed world locations, expansion order is likely to be the single biggest strategic decision in the early game.

Community reports from the Beta suggest players gravitate toward production facilities first and comfort facilities later. That is player experience, not developer guidance, but it lines up with how the survival loop is described: resources in, survival capacity out.

## Resources: What You Scavenge and What You Refine

Six materials are named in the current documentation: branches, logs, charcoal, metal, cloth, and plastic. A metal-scrap refinement chain is described in developer interviews, and wood and plastic reportedly follow similar multi-step processing.

| Raw input | Intermediate | Refined output | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metal scraps | Slag metal | Pure metal | Developer-described chain |
| Wood | Not published | Not published | Described as following similar refining steps |
| Plastic | Not published | Not published | Described as following similar refining steps |
| Branches, logs, charcoal | — | Fuel | Discussed as Eden Engine fuel |
| Cloth | — | Clothing | Used for cold-mitigation gear |

The absence of published yields is the biggest blind spot in Frostrail crafting right now. You cannot plan a precise material budget, so plan in ratios instead: bank a healthy surplus of whatever your refinery consumes before you commit to a long northern push. If the scrap-to-slag-to-pure-metal pattern holds, expect each tier to cost more input than the last, which makes bulk scavenging strictly better than opportunistic grabbing.

Fuel deserves separate attention. Branches, logs, and charcoal are tied to the Eden Engine, and the train does not move without it. In practice that means every crafting session competes with travel for the same wood pile — a tension the game seems designed to create on purpose.

## Frostrail Crafting for Weapons: Inside the Assembly Station

Weapon production appears to be the showcase use case for Frostrail crafting. The Assembly Station interface, shown officially, presents a modular rifle with swappable parts rather than a single fixed item. Only two rifles have been named in developer interviews, while community Beta records add pistol and melee names.

| Weapon | Source of the name | Described role | Published stats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pipe Rifle | Developer interviews | Early-game weapon | None |
| Bolt-Action Rifle | Official Assembly Station screenshots | Modular firearm example | None |
| Pistol and melee names | Community Beta records | Player-reported | None |

Damage, ammo type, rarity, magazine size, range, and weight are all unpublished. That matters because the same documentation stresses that ammunition is intentionally valuable and that combat is meant to feel serious. A modular gun you can rebuild at the bench is therefore less about raw power and more about logistics: you keep one platform running instead of chasing three.

If you are coming from other survival games, the mental shift is to treat ammo as a crafted currency rather than loot. Craft in small batches, keep a reserve for the trip home, and never strip your last stack of components to build something you'll replace in two stops.

## Clothing, Food, and Systems That Touch the Bench

Not everything adjacent to Frostrail crafting is a bench recipe, but the systems overlap enough that they belong in the same planning conversation.

| System | Crafting connection | What is actually known |
|---|---|---|
| Clothing | Crafted cold-mitigation gear | A community Beta record for a Felt Jacket lists a recipe plus reported cold-resistance and movement fields |
| Food | Cooking station output | Food can improve health and other statistics; some may help against cold |
| Relics | Not crafted | Found in dungeons, activated aboard the train for temporary buffs |

Clothing is the clearest example of a system where the community has moved ahead of official documentation. The Felt Jacket entry circulating from Beta testing is player experience and should be treated as a snapshot of one build, not a permanent recipe. Still, its existence confirms the shape of the system: cold exposure is a real threat, and the answer is a bench, not a lucky find.

Relics sit outside the crafting loop by design. They are dungeon rewards that grant temporary buffs when activated on the train, which makes them a complement to production rather than a replacement for it. Individual relic names and effect values remain unpublished.

## Practical Tips for Planning Around the Bench

- **Build production before comfort.** A refinery that never stops working beats a nicer carriage every time.
- **Bank fuel separately.** Keep Eden Engine fuel in its own mental budget so crafting sessions don't strand the train.
- **Refine in bulk, not on demand.** Multi-step chains punish small batches; run the refinery when you have surplus, not when you're desperate.
- **Keep one weapon platform.** Modular assembly rewards depth over breadth, especially with ammo treated as valuable.
- **Craft cold gear before the northern push.** Community reports point to clothing as the main defense against exposure.
- **Explore for inputs, not finished goods.** Dungeons supply Relics; the bench supplies everything else.
- **Log your own numbers.** Since official yields are unpublished, your own notes are the best data available to you.

## FAQ

**Is Frostrail crafting available in the current build?**
Benches, refineries, a cooking station, and the Assembly Station are all described or shown, but the game is in pre-release and recipe counts, processing times, and station costs have not been published. Expect values to shift between builds.

**What is the most important material for Frostrail crafting?**
Metal scraps, because they feed the only fully described refinement chain: scraps to slag metal to pure metal. Wood and plastic reportedly follow similar multi-step processing, but their intermediate names and yields are unpublished.

**Can you craft weapons from scratch?**
The Assembly Station is presented as a modular firearm interface, with the Bolt-Action Rifle used as the official example and the Pipe Rifle described as an early-game option. Exact build requirements and stat values remain unknown.

**Where should I look for reliable Frostrail crafting information?**
Start with official screenshots and developer interviews, then cross-check community Beta records and label them as player experience. Anything quoting precise recipe counts or processing times without a build date should be treated as provisional.
