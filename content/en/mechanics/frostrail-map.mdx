## What the Frostrail Map Covers (and What It Doesn't)

The Frostrail map is not a single illustrated atlas you can pin to the wall — at least not yet. Frostrail is a frozen-wasteland survival game built around a train pushing north through connected levels, and its map is really a network of location types, branch points, and exits that you uncover as you go. That distinction matters because every stop on the Frostrail map trades warmth and time for supplies, weapons, Relics, or a safer line onward.

The good news is that nine location types are documented, and the developers have been unusually clear about how the world is assembled. The bad news is that named regions, map coordinates, and a finished world atlas are not published. Anyone selling you a complete Frostrail map with labeled zones is working from guesswork.

The frozen north itself is the game's principal open biome. The May 2026 update expanded the tundra for further exploration, and blizzards there reduce both visibility and movement — two penalties that stack badly when you are already low on warmth. New points of interest are being built by hand rather than generated purely by algorithm, which is a meaningful detail for route planning: hand-authored content tends to stay put between builds, while procedural placement is the part that shifts.

| Element | Confirmed | Not published |
|---|---|---|
| Open biome | Tundra, expanded in the May 2026 update | Region boundaries and coordinates |
| Location types | Nine documented types | Individual site names |
| Structure | Connected levels advancing north with branching exits | A complete world atlas |
| Terrain | Hand-authored foundations plus procedural POIs, loot, and enemies | Fixed layouts between builds |
| Weather | Blizzards cut visibility and movement | Storm timing or forecast systems |
| Demo | Three explorable areas, October 19–26, 2026 | The names of those three areas |

## The Nine Location Types on the Frostrail Map

Every documented stop falls into one of nine categories, and each one pays out differently. Knowing which category a silhouette belongs to is the closest thing to reading a legend that the current build offers.

| Location type | What it is for | Relative risk | Official detail available |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tundra | Open travel between stops | Moderate (weather) | Biome confirmed; no coordinates |
| Abandoned settlements | Supplies, equipment, environmental storytelling | Low to moderate | No settlement names or guaranteed loot |
| Wreckage | Salvage beside the rails | Low | No loot tables |
| Mining camps | Forage stop for food and materials | Moderate | No canonical names |
| Villages | Forage stop for food and materials | Moderate | No canonical names |
| Modular dungeons | Relic expeditions | High | Layouts vary; still under development |
| Subterranean vaults | High-reward interiors | High | Rewards described as powerful weapons, not itemized |
| Derelict bunkers | Enclosed exploration | High | Access requirements, enemy and loot tables unpublished |
| Ruined strongholds and frozen ruins | High-risk ruins | High | Placement and reward structure unpublished |

Modular dungeons are the standout. They were overhauled with updated lighting, they use modular construction so layouts can vary between runs, and they are a known source of Relics — which can be activated aboard the train rather than hauled around as dead weight. An official screenshot shows the objective "The Trial Below — Search the unknown for a hidden shrine," and the April test brief explicitly included early-game dungeon raids. In other words, the map is not gated behind a long overworld grind before your first Relic run.

Abandoned settlements and wreckage are the low-friction stops. The official store description names them as exploration and scavenging spaces used to recover supplies and equipment, and environmental storytelling is baked into the frozen-empire premise. Individual settlement names and guaranteed loot are unpublished, so treat them as reliable categories rather than reliable addresses.

Mining camps and villages are forage stops, not a finished world atlas. Food and materials are the stated finds. Subterranean vaults sit alongside them in the documentation and promise powerful weapons, though the rewards are not itemized and their relationship to modular dungeons has not been separately mapped. Derelict bunkers and ruined strongholds round out the high-risk tier, with threats, Relics, and resources all still under wraps.

## How the Frostrail Map Branches North

Progress is not a straight line with a boss at the end. The journey advances north through connected levels, and crews can switch tracks, clear obstacles, and choose among exits or tunnels leading onward. Each of those is a different kind of decision with a different price.

| Decision | What it costs | What it can return |
|---|---|---|
| Switch tracks | Time; possibly a longer route | An alternate line with different stops |
| Clear an obstacle | Time and combat risk | Access to a blocked branch |
| Enter an exit or tunnel | Commitment to a path | Progress north |
| Detour to a point of interest | Warmth and time | Supplies, equipment, Relics |
| Raid a dungeon or vault | Warmth and high combat risk | Relics and powerful weapons |

A developer interview notes that thorough exploration can push a run past two hours, though final level duration is not fixed. Treat that figure as a planning tool rather than a promise: it tells you the Frostrail map rewards curiosity, and it tells you that a completionist pace is slow by design. If you are the kind of player who opens every door, budget your warmth accordingly.

## Route Planning: Warmth, Time, and Risk

Warmth and time are the two currencies the Frostrail map actually charges you in, and they are not interchangeable. A detour that costs ten minutes of blizzard exposure is far more expensive than one that costs ten minutes of clear rail. Blizzards reduce visibility and movement simultaneously, so a route that looks short on paper can turn out to be the longest one on the board.

The practical rule is to decide what you are shopping for before you commit to a branch. Interior locations — dungeons, vaults, bunkers, strongholds — pay in quality but charge in combat risk and warmth. Surface stops — settlements, wreckage, camps — pay in volume and charge mostly time. Mixing the two in one sortie is where crews usually get into trouble.

| If you need... | Prioritize | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Food and materials | Mining camps, villages | Stated forage finds |
| General supplies and gear | Abandoned settlements, wreckage | Documented scavenging spaces |
| Relics | Modular dungeons | Known Relic source, activatable on the train |
| Heavy weapons | Subterranean vaults | Rewards described as powerful weapons |
| Speed | Rail-hugging route, skip interiors | Warmth and time are the currency |

## Practical Tips for Reading the Frostrail Map

- **Treat the tundra as a cost, not a shortcut.** Blizzards cut visibility and movement at the same time, so open ground is rarely the fast option.
- **Bank Relics early.** Relics recovered from dungeons can be activated aboard the train, so one successful raid compounds for the rest of the run.
- **Re-scout after every update.** New points of interest are artist-built and dungeon layouts remain under development; community reports from early tests describe layouts shifting between runs.
- **Do not over-trust the demo.** Three explorable areas are a sample, not a world atlas.
- **Keep your own landmark log.** With no published coordinates, your notes are the only Frostrail map written in your own handwriting.
- **Pick your exit before you loot.** Deciding which tunnel you are leaving through prevents the classic mistake of a full inventory and no way out.

## FAQ

**Is there an official Frostrail map with named regions and coordinates?**
No. The developers have not published named regions, map coordinates, or a finished world atlas. What exists is a documented set of nine location types plus a level structure that branches north through connected areas. The Frostrail map will likely fill in closer to launch, but for now it is a framework rather than a picture.

**How many location types are on the Frostrail map?**
Nine, as of the October 8, 2026 documentation update: tundra, modular dungeons, abandoned settlements, wreckage, mining camps, villages, subterranean vaults, derelict bunkers, and ruined strongholds or frozen ruins.

**Where should I go first for Relics?**
Modular dungeons are the known Relic source, and the April test brief included early-game dungeon raids — so you are not expected to grind the surface first. Remember that Relics can be activated aboard the train, which means a successful raid is worth more than the loot you carry out.

**Is the demo map the full game world?**
No. The Next Fest demo running October 19–26, 2026 lists three explorable areas alongside train operation, scavenging, crafting, and Revenant combat. Individual area names are not listed, and the demo sample is explicitly not a complete world atlas. Expect the final Frostrail map to be larger and considerably more detailed.
