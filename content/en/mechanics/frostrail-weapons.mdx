## Why Frostrail Weapons Are a Resource Decision, Not a Stat Sheet

Frostrail drops you onto an armored train crossing a frozen former empire, and it hands you a rule most survival games ignore: bullets are expensive. The developers have described combat as deliberately serious and ammunition as intentionally valuable, which reframes Frostrail weapons as an economic choice rather than a damage race. Every shot you fire is a material you have to replace somewhere out on the tundra.

That framing matters because the game is still pre-release. A lot of what circulates about Frostrail weapons is either dated, tied to a specific test build, or simply not published yet. This guide sticks to what developers have actually said, flags community reports as community reports, and shows you how to prepare a loadout without inventing numbers that don't exist.

## Modular Assembly: How Frostrail Weapons Get Built

The most concrete mechanical detail is the Assembly Station, an onboard workbench where firearms are put together from modular parts. That design choice tells you a lot about how Frostrail weapons will likely behave in practice: your rifle is a collection of components, so upgrading it may mean swapping a part rather than finding a strictly better gun.

Crafting benches and refineries can be added to the train as it expands, which turns your locomotive into a moving production line. Materials feed that line through a developer-described refinement chain, and the intermediate steps matter because raw scrap is not directly usable.

| Raw material | Intermediate step | Refined output | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metal scraps | Slag metal | Pure metal | Developer-described chain |
| Wood | Similar refining steps | Refined wood | Exact yields unpublished |
| Plastic | Similar refining steps | Refined plastic | Exact yields unpublished |
| Branches, logs, charcoal | — | Fuel | Discussed as Eden Engine fuel |
| Cloth | — | — | Tied to clothing and cold mitigation |

Two practical takeaways follow. First, hoard metal scraps early — the chain has at least two conversion steps before you reach usable material. Second, keep the Eden Engine fed, because a stalled train means no workbench, no refining, and no way to rebuild the Frostrail weapons you burned through on the last expedition.

## Ammo Scarcity and Shot Discipline

Because ammunition is intentionally valuable, the strongest weapon skill in Frostrail isn't aim — it's restraint. A fight you avoid entirely costs nothing. A fight you take and abandon halfway costs ammo and health and leaves a wounded enemy behind.

- Count engagements, not kills: decide before you fire whether you can finish the group.
- Keep a melee option equipped so cleanup doesn't burn bullets.
- Sweep locations after combat; scavenging is described as a core equipment source.
- Bank spare ammo on the train before long dungeon runs rather than carrying everything.
- Treat every shot at a Revenant pack as a budget decision, because Revenants are described as larger-group melee pressure.

That last point is the real reason Frostrail weapons deserve a plan. Enemy design pushes you toward volume of fire at close range, while the ammo economy punishes exactly that behavior. The tension between the two is the combat loop.

Different threats ask different things of your loadout, and the published enemy descriptions are specific enough to plan around even without stat blocks.

| Threat | Reported behavior | Loadout implication |
|---|---|---|
| Revenants | Larger-group melee pressure | Sustained close-range output; watch ammo burn |

| Corrupted Soldiers | Planned ranged enemies | Cover discipline and a longer-range option |
| Void-linked horrors | Eldritch threats, tone of tension and action | Unknown scaling; scout before committing |

Note that clothing is a weapon-support system in practice. The community-reported Felt Jacket record includes cold-resistance and movement fields, and any gear that keeps you moving outdoors effectively extends how long you can carry a heavier firearm before turning back.

Relics belong in the same conversation. They're found in dungeons and activated aboard the train for temporary buffs, so a relic that improves combat performance can substitute for a weapon upgrade you haven't crafted yet.

## A Practical Pre-Run Workflow for Frostrail Weapons

Use this sequence before every expedition. It assumes nothing about final balance and works with whatever the current build offers.

1. **Check the workbench.** Confirm which modular parts are actually attached to your rifle before you leave.
2. **Refine first, craft second.** Convert metal scraps into slag metal and then pure metal while the train is stationary.
3. **Set an ammo budget.** Decide how many rounds you're willing to spend, then leave the surplus in storage.
4. **Pack a melee backup.** Community reports suggest melee options exist; they preserve ammo on weak targets.
5. **Dress for the route.** Cold mitigation clothing is non-negotiable for outdoor stretches.
6. **Scout the exit.** Multiple exits can lead to the next destination, so choose the route that matches your loadout.

| Phase | Weapon priority | Supporting system |
|---|---|---|
| First hours | Get any functional firearm, likely the Pipe Rifle | Basic scavenging |
| Early expeditions | Learn the Assembly Station interface | Crafting bench on the train |
| Mid-game | Refine metal and tune modular parts | Refineries and fuel supply |
| Dungeon runs | Carry relic buffs plus a reliable sidearm | Relics activated on the train |

Community reports consistently describe the Beta as a system-learning experience rather than a balanced game, so treat any Frostrail weapons advice from that period as provisional. The developer-facing material is clear that values are still moving.

## FAQ

**Are there official damage stats for Frostrail weapons yet?**
No. Damage, ammo type, rarity, magazine size, range, and weight are all listed as unpublished. Anything with precise numbers is either from a specific test build or guesswork.

**How many Frostrail weapons have been named?**
Officially, two rifles: the Pipe Rifle and the Bolt-Action Rifle. Community Beta records add a pistol and melee entries, but those aren't developer-confirmed names.

**What should I craft first for combat?**
Focus on the material chain before the weapon itself. Metal scraps convert to slag metal and then pure metal, and that refined metal is the foundation for the modular parts your Frostrail weapons are built from.

**Does melee still matter if I have a rifle?**
Yes, for practical reasons. With ammo described as intentionally valuable, a melee option lets you finish weak enemies and save rounds for Revenant groups and ranged Corrupted Soldiers.

**Will the modular system replace finding new guns?**
That's the implication of the Assembly Station, but it hasn't been confirmed. Treat modular assembly and scavenged finds as two paths that may both exist in the final build.
