## The Core Loop: Prep Aboard, Loot Below

The cold in Frostrail rarely kills you in a dramatic charge. It kills you quietly, while your crew is three rooms deep in a frozen chapel and the cab temperature is already sliding past −28 °C. Your only real lifeline is the Eden Engine, a locomotive that has to be fueled, driven, maintained, and defended while you scavenge a hostile world for materials and a route north.

That split — mobile base versus frozen expedition — is the whole game, and it is why this Frostrail survival guide starts aboard the train rather than in the snow. Every run alternates between preparing on the Engine and pushing into levels for equipment, relics, and exits. Get the preparation wrong and the exploration phase becomes a slow walk back to a dead engine.

The good news for new crews: progression now carries between levels in the current development build, so a rough expedition does not automatically erase your work. That makes experimentation cheaper — but it does not make a destroyed Eden Engine survivable, because a broken or destroyed locomotive still ends the run.

### Pre-Departure Checklist

| Phase | What You Are Doing | Common Mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Stoke and check heat | Confirm the Eden Engine is burning before anyone leaves | Leaving with a low fire and no fuel reserve |
| Inventory audit | Count ammunition, food, and repair materials | Carrying loot instead of tools |
| Route selection | Pick among the multiple exits each level offers | Taking the first exit instead of scouting |
| Expedition | Scavenge, fight Revenants, find relics | Overextending past the point of safe return |
| Return and refine | Break down materials at onboard stations | Hoarding raw scraps you could refine |

## Survival Basics: Heat, Food, and Clothing

Heat is the one survival requirement that actually functions like a meter. The developers have framed warmth around the Eden Engine rather than a generic body-temperature bar, which means your safety radius is the train — and anything that pulls you away from it is a calculated risk. Blizzards are the obvious threat, but the more dangerous scenario is a long interior crawl where you lose track of distance and return to a cold cab.

Food works differently than most survival players expect. It grants buffs rather than feeding a mandatory hunger meter, so eating is a tactical decision, not a chore. Save the strong buffs for Revenant fights or long expeditions, and treat ordinary food as expedition fuel rather than something you must top off constantly.

Clothing is your portable version of heat. Multiple new clothing sets were reported in the May 2026 update, which gives crews real choices about how they build for a trip.

| Need | Where It Comes From | What It Actually Does | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heat | Eden Engine, cab fire | Core survival requirement; keeps the run alive | Critical |
| Fuel | Logs, branches, charcoal | Feeds the engine and the heat it produces | Critical |
| Clothing | Found or crafted sets | Extends how far you can travel from the cab | High |
| Food | Scavenged and prepared | Buffs rather than a hunger meter | Medium |
| Ammunition | Scavenged and crafted | Combat endurance against Revenants | High |
| Repair materials | Refined metal and wood | Keeps the Engine and guns working | High |

A simple rule from player experience: never leave the train with less than a full fuel reserve for the return trip. Crews that die in the cold usually die because they spent the fuel budget on the way out.

## Combat and Ammunition Discipline

Frostrail is guns-first. The arsenal is WWI-influenced — pistols, shotguns, rifles, machine guns — with basic melee options as a fallback. Ranged weapons also carry a secondary melee attack, which matters enormously when a Revenant closes the gap while you are mid-reload.

All guns are planned to be craftable and modular, which means your weapon quality is a production problem as much as a combat one. Exact damage, ammunition, rarity, and balance values have not been officially published, so treat any specific number you see online with suspicion. Community reports consistently describe ammunition as scarce, and that scarcity should shape how you fight.

| Weapon Class | Best Use | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| Pistols | Backup, tight interiors | Low sustained damage |
| Shotguns | Stopping Revenants at close range | Punishing if you miss |
| Rifles | Picking threats before they close | Slower follow-up shots |
| Machine guns | Holding a corridor or carriage | Ammo-hungry |
| Melee / secondary attacks | Finishing wounded enemies | Risky against groups |

Practical habits that keep ammo in the crate:

- Fire from doorways and stairs so Revenants funnel into one lane.
- Finish wounded enemies with the secondary melee attack instead of a second shell.
- Split ammo types across the crew so one death does not empty the reserve.
- Retreat to the Engine rather than winning a fight you cannot resupply after.

## Running the Eden Engine

The Eden Engine is simultaneously your heater, your storage, your crafting bench, and your escape route. It is also a fail state. Crews must fuel, power, drive, maintain, expand, and defend it, which means somebody always has a job even when the scavenging team is idle.

Fuel inputs discussed so far include logs, branches, and charcoal — a hierarchy that rewards you for processing wood rather than burning raw scavenge. Planned upgrades cover walls, hatches, doors, stations, storage, and carriages, so the train is meant to grow into a mobile fortress over a run.

| Upgrade Category | Why It Matters | Suggested Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Walls, hatches, doors | Surviving attacks on the train itself | High |
| Storage | Carrying more between levels | High |
| Stations | Crafting, modification, and repair | High |
| Carriages | Expanding capacity and workspace | Medium |
| Fuel handling | Keeping heat stable on long trips | Critical |

Defense deserves its own mention. If Revenants can reach the Engine, they can end a run that took hours to build, and the crew's best fighters may be halfway across the map. Assign at least one player to stay near the train during risky pushes, or accept that you are gambling your entire save on a fast return.

## Crafting, Repair, and Co-op Crew Roles

Crafting links scavenged material to everything else: weapons, train improvements, equipment, and dedicated onboard workstations. The refinement chains are the part new players underestimate. Metal scraps do not become a rifle directly — they refine into slag metal and then pure metal. Wood and plastic follow comparable multi-step chains.

| Material | Refinement Chain | Typical Output |
|---|---|---|
| Metal scraps | Scraps → slag metal → pure metal | Guns, train parts, repairs |
| Wood | Multi-step refinement | Fuel, structures, crafting stock |
| Plastic | Comparable refinement chain | Equipment and components |

Weapon modification and repair happen at dedicated onboard workstations, which reinforces the pattern of the whole game: the train is where value is created, and the frozen levels are where the raw material comes from. If your workstations are idle, your crew is leaving power on the table.

Crew size shapes everything. Steam lists both single-player and online co-op, and the official FAQ confirms crews of one to four, so the division of labor changes dramatically between runs.

| Crew Size | Suggested Split | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 player | Alternate short expeditions with engine maintenance | Keep trips close to the cab |
| 2 players | One engineer/driver, one scout | Scout never carries all the ammo |
| 3 players | Engineer, scavenger, guard | Guard covers the return leg |
| 4 players | Engineer, two scavengers, quartermaster | Quartermaster manages fuel, food, and ammo |

## Frostrail Survival Guide FAQ

**Is Frostrail single-player or co-op?**
Both. Steam lists single-player and online co-op, and the official FAQ confirms crews of one to four players. A solo run is viable, but you will be doing engine maintenance and scavenging with the same pair of hands.

**What is the most important system in a Frostrail survival guide?**
Heat and the Eden Engine. Warmth is the survival requirement, the Engine produces it, and a destroyed Engine ends the run. Fuel, defense, and repairs all feed back into that one system.

**Does food work like a hunger meter?**
No. Food grants buffs instead of filling a mandatory hunger bar, so you can treat eating as a tactical choice before fights or long expeditions rather than constant upkeep.

**Can you lose an entire run?**
Yes. A broken or destroyed Eden Engine is a fail state. Progression currently carries between levels, which softens individual setbacks, but losing the train itself is still the end of that run.

**Should I burn raw wood or refine it first?**
Community reports favor processing wood where possible and keeping charcoal for emergencies. Logs and branches keep the fire alive, but refined fuel gives you a safer margin on long expeditions.
