## What a Four-Player Crew Actually Does

Joining friends in Frostrail isn't just about getting into the same session — it's about what you do once the train starts moving. The co-op loop asks crews to coordinate fuel, power, driving, repair, and train defense, and to split scavenging from guard duty.

| Role | Core job | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Driver / engineer | Manage throttle, power, and momentum | Engine noise has no confirmed aggro radius — don't plan around a number |
| Quartermaster | Track fuel, ammo, and crafting materials | Resources are scarce and shared |
| Scavenger | Leave the train to gather supplies | Know your exit routes before you commit |
| Guard | Hold the carriage while others loot | Coordinate timing with the scavengers |

### Tips That Make Random Crews Work

- Decide who is responsible for fuel before the first departure, not during a crisis.
- Split scavenging and train-guard tasks so the locomotive is never completely unattended.
- Share ammunition and crafting resources deliberately — hoarding breaks the loop.
- Choose between multiple level exits as a group; splitting up at an exit is how crews get separated.
- Use a third-party voice client until in-game voice chat is confirmed.

Community reports around the test period consistently point to the same lesson: the crews that talked the most about fuel and exits survived the longest. Player experience also suggests that treating engine noise as a known "aggro radius" is a mistake, since no such value has ever been published.
